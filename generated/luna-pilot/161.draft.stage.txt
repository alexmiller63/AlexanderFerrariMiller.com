# NGC 1261: A Quiet Beacon in the Southern Sky

*An old, compact city of stars waits low in the southern heavens. Here’s how to find it—and what its catalog numbers can tell you.*

Some celestial landmarks announce themselves at a glance. NGC 1261 asks for a little patience. In the far southern sky, this globular cluster is a faint, rounded glow: not a bright naked-eye showpiece, but a rewarding destination for a careful search.

Its position is listed as right ascension 03h 12m 16.21s and declination −55° 12′ 58.4″ (J2000). SIMBAD classifies it as a globular cluster and lists a visual magnitude of 8.63 and an apparent diameter of 6.9 arcminutes. Those figures describe a concentrated but extended target—not a pinpoint of light. [SIMBAD’s NGC 1261 entry](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=NGC%201261)

A globular cluster is a gravitationally bound gathering of stars, typically packed into a roughly spherical swarm. The stars crowd most closely toward the center, while fainter members extend outward. Through a telescope, that structure can make the difference between seeing a soft patch and beginning to glimpse texture. The catalog classification tells us what kind of object NGC 1261 is; its listed brightness and size help set expectations for the view.
