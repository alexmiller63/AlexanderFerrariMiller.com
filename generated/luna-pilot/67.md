# Gamma Ursae Minoris: A Star in the Catalog

A star’s catalog entry can be brief, but it is not empty. The SIMBAD record for HIP 75097 identifies it as “gam UMi” and classifies it as a Delta Scuti variable. It also reports a spectral type, position, parallax, motion, velocity and measured brightnesses—a compact set of facts that describes how astronomers have recorded this object, while leaving some questions unanswered. ([SIMBAD: HIP 75097](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2075097))

The position listed for the star is right ascension 15h 20m 43.704s and declination +71° 50′ 02.49″, in the J2000 coordinate system. Its high positive declination places the listed position far north of the celestial equator. SIMBAD attributes these coordinates to the 2020 Hipparcos and Gaia catalog compilation. The same compilation gives the star a parallax of 6.5959 milliarcseconds, with an uncertainty of 0.2943 milliarcseconds. Parallax is the apparent positional shift used to infer a star’s distance; the record supplies the measured value, rather than a distance estimate. ([SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2075097); Gaia catalog, [2020yCat.1350....0G](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020yCat.1350....0G))

The entry also records proper motion: −15.908 milliarcseconds per year in right ascension, with the declination correction included, and +17.146 milliarcseconds per year in declination. These measurements describe the star’s apparent movement across the sky. They do not, by themselves, tell a reader how that movement would look without a time interval and a suitable comparison. SIMBAD lists the proper-motion data from the same 2020 catalog compilation. ([SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2075097); [2020yCat.1350....0G](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020yCat.1350....0G))

Other measurements add to the portrait. SIMBAD gives a heliocentric radial velocity of −3.90 kilometers per second, with an uncertainty of 0.6 kilometers per second, citing a 2006 publication. It lists the spectral type as A2III, with a quality code of C, and cites a 1995 source for that classification. These entries are not equally precise: the database explicitly includes quality codes, as well as uncertainties where reported. ([SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2075097); radial velocity, [2006AstL...32..759G](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006AstL...32..759G); spectral type, [1995ApJS...99..135A](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1995ApJS...99..135A))

The variable-star label is a notable part of the record, but the supplied entry does not give a period, amplitude or observing history for the variation. It identifies HIP 75097 as a Delta Scuti variable; it does not explain how the classification was established or detail the star’s changing brightness. That distinction matters: a catalog label is useful evidence about an object’s recorded classification, not a substitute for all the observations behind it. ([SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2075097))

SIMBAD lists visual magnitudes of 3.002 in V and 3.067 in B, both attributed to a 2000 publication. It also includes measurements in the G, J, H and K bands, drawn from later catalog sources. Each value is a measurement in a particular band, not a complete account of the star’s behavior. Read together, the entries make HIP 75097 more than a name: they offer a carefully sourced snapshot, with both the star’s recorded properties and the limits of what this record alone can tell us.
