# Jabbah: A Many-Star Spark in Scorpius

*Look toward the Scorpion’s forehead for a modest point of light with a more complicated identity than the unaided eye can reveal.*

Jabbah is easy to mistake for a single, ordinary star. In the Scorpion’s crowded head, it shines around fourth magnitude: visible to the naked eye under a dark sky, but not so bright that it demands attention. Its quiet appearance is part of the pleasure. A telescope turns that unassuming dot into a small investigation.

The name belongs to Nu Scorpii, which SIMBAD classifies as a double or multiple star. Its catalog entry gives a visual magnitude of about 4.0 and a spectral type of B2V. That “B” marks a hot, blue-white star; the “V” identifies a main-sequence star, still shining through the steady work of fusing hydrogen in its core. The cataloged parallax is 6.88 milliarcseconds—an apparent shift in position as Earth travels around the Sun. Taken at face value, it places the system roughly 470 light-years away, though the listed measurement has uncertainty. [SIMBAD: HIP 79374 / Nu Scorpii](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2079374)

At that distance, the view we receive is a blend of starlight crossing a great stretch of space. The naked eye collects it as one point. The word *multiple* hints at a different story: more than one star belongs to the system, even if a casual glance cannot separate them. The challenge for an observer is to move from the broad pattern of the constellation to the finer pattern hidden inside one of its points.
