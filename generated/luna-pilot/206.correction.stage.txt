# NGC 2655: A Galaxy with Two Classifications

NGC 2655 does not need a dramatic description to invite a closer look. Its catalogue record offers a more measured story: one classification describes its overall form, while another identifies activity at its center. Together, the labels make this galaxy a reminder that a single object can be characterized in more than one way.

The SIMBAD astronomical database identifies NGC 2655 as a Seyfert 2 galaxy. It also records its morphological type as E-S0, a classification reported in a 2018 study. The two labels address different properties: Seyfert 2 is the galaxy’s listed activity class, while E-S0 is its morphological class. Neither label, on its own, supplies a complete account of the galaxy.

The database gives NGC 2655 an optical angular size of about 5.02 by 4.04 arcminutes, based on a 2023 catalogue entry. That measurement describes how large the galaxy appears on the sky, not its physical diameter. Its catalogue record also includes a visual magnitude of 11.08, drawn from a 2010 photometry reference. These figures provide ways to compare observations, but they do not tell us what a particular observer will see through a particular instrument or under particular sky conditions.

Another entry places NGC 2655’s measured redshift at about 0.00485, with an uncertainty of 0.0005. SIMBAD lists the measurement as optical and attributes it to a 2022 study. Redshift is a reported measurement, not a direct description of the galaxy’s appearance. The database also gives a radial velocity of 1,452 kilometers per second, with an uncertainty of 150 kilometers per second, from the same reference. These values belong to the galaxy’s catalogue record alongside its classifications and size.

The distinctions matter. A morphological label and an activity label are not competing verdicts: they organize information about different aspects of the same object. Likewise, an angular measurement and a brightness entry describe observational quantities rather than explaining what gives the galaxy its classifications. Keeping those categories separate makes the record more useful—and avoids asking any one number to tell the whole story.

SIMBAD lists NGC 2655’s J2000 infrared coordinates as right ascension 08h 55m 37.731s and declination +78° 13′ 23.10″. The database attributes those coordinates to a 2006 reference. It also lists photometric measurements in several bands, including near-infrared J, H and K values and optical g, r and z values. Each entry is tied to its own catalogue reference, underscoring that astronomical records are assembled from measurements made in different ways and at different wavelengths.

For a reader encountering NGC 2655 in a catalogue, the essential portrait is therefore concise but layered: a galaxy classified as Seyfert 2, assigned an E-S0 morphological type, measured at several arcminutes across in optical data, and listed with a small positive redshift. Those facts do not turn the galaxy into a spectacle, nor do they need to. They show how careful observation builds a portrait from distinct pieces of evidence.
