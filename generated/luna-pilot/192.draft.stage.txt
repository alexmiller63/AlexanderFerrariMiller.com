# Gemini Nebula; 2372? The planetary nebula with a question mark

*At the edge of Gemini, a faint, compact cloud marks a star’s afterlife—and rewards patient looking.*

The name in our heading is a question, not a catalog verdict. The object discussed here is **NGC 2371**: SIMBAD classifies it as a planetary nebula and gives its J2000 position as 7h 25m 34.7s, +29° 29′ 26″. The linked catalog entry does not confirm “2372” as an alternate designation, so check your chart before setting out. [SIMBAD: NGC 2371](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=NGC%202371)

“Planetary” is a historical misnomer. Through early telescopes, some of these small, roundish clouds looked like planets. In fact, a planetary nebula is a shell of gas cast off by a star in its late stages. Radiation from the hot stellar remnant energizes the surrounding gas, making it glow. The shell is brief on cosmic timescales; the star’s life, and the nebula’s slow expansion, unfold over thousands of years.

NGC 2371 is a small target on the sky. SIMBAD lists an angular size of about 0.73 arcminutes, with the measurement marked as radio, and a visual magnitude entry of 13.5. Those figures are useful guides, not promises of what an observer will see: the view depends on aperture, sky darkness, magnification and observing conditions. A nebula can be more difficult to pick out than a star
