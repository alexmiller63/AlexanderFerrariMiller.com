# NGC 6543: A Portrait in Catalog Data

NGC 6543 is listed in SIMBAD as a planetary nebula. Its catalog entry offers a compact portrait assembled from measurements: a position in the sky, an angular size, a parallax, a radial velocity, and recorded brightness values. Taken together, these numbers describe how astronomers identify and characterize the object, while also showing how much of a portrait depends on what was measured and how it was recorded.

The listed J2000 position is right ascension 17h 58m 33.404s and declination +66° 37′ 58.751″. SIMBAD marks the coordinates as optical and gives them an A quality flag. That flag is part of the database’s coordinate system: its notes define A as the best quality category, associated here with Gaia measurements that include proper motions. The position is precise enough to distinguish the object on a chart, but it is not the only coordinate information in the entry. SIMBAD also supplies a B1950 position and Galactic coordinates, each using a different coordinate framework.

The angular size in the record is 0.623 by 0.536 arcminutes, measured in the near-infrared. Converted to arcseconds, that is approximately 37.4 by 32.2 arcseconds. The catalog gives an orientation angle of 10 degrees and a C quality flag for this size measurement. Those details matter: the dimensions are not a single universal figure, but a reported measurement tied to a wavelength range and a reference.

Distance is represented by parallax. SIMBAD lists a parallax of 0.7321 milliarcseconds, with a stated error of 0.0287 milliarcseconds and an A quality flag. Taking the reciprocal of the parallax gives an approximate distance of 1,366 parsecs. That is a calculation from the catalog value, not a distance directly stated in the entry; the quoted measurement uncertainty also means the result should not be treated as exact.

The record includes motion as well. Its proper motion is −0.434 milliarcseconds per year in right ascension, with the cosine-of-declination convention specified by SIMBAD, and −6.532 milliarcseconds per year in declination. The listed quality is A. Separately, the entry reports a radial velocity of −65.7 kilometers per second, with an uncertainty of 0.9 kilometers per second and an E quality flag. These are distinct catalog quantities: one describes apparent motion across the sky, while the other is the listed velocity along the line of sight.

SIMBAD also records the spectral type as [WC], with a D quality flag. Its brightness entries span several bands: B 11.09, V 11.28, G 11.237219, J 8.953, H 9.076, and K 8.340. The values come from different cited records, and the entry provides uncertainties for them. Rather than one all-purpose brightness number, the catalog preserves measurements in separate bands and connects them to their sources.

NGC 6543’s entry is therefore more than a name attached to a point in the sky. It is a set of measurements with units, reference systems, quality labels, and citations. Read carefully, it shows both what the database records and the limits of any single number: position depends on a coordinate frame, size on the measurement wavelength, and distance on interpreting a parallax.
