# Brachium, Libra’s changing ember

*An ancient constellation holds a red giant whose light refuses to stay still—and a good reason to return to the same patch of sky.*

Some stars are landmarks because they keep their place in familiar patterns. Brachium offers another kind of invitation: come back, and the star may not look quite the same. Also cataloged as Sigma Librae (σ Librae), it lies in Libra, the zodiac constellation, and is classified as a Mira variable. That makes it a changing point of light in a sky we often treat as fixed. [SIMBAD: HIP 73714](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2073714)

The catalog places Brachium at right ascension 15 hours 04 minutes and declination 25 degrees 17 minutes south. Its measured parallax is about 12.54 milliarcseconds. Parallax is the apparent shift of a nearby star against more distant background stars as Earth moves around the Sun; converting that measurement gives a distance of roughly 260 light-years. The same catalog lists a spectral type of M2.5 III: an M-type star, relatively cool compared with the Sun, and a giant. [SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2073714)

The Mira classification is the key to watching Brachium. Mira variables are pulsating red giants: their outer layers expand and contract, and their brightness changes over time. The SIMBAD entry identifies Brachium as a Mira variable, but the data supplied there do not give a current brightness forecast or a period to plan around. So make the observation an experiment rather than a test of whether the star matches a number. Look for it, estimate how bright it seems, and return on another clear night. A sequence of impressions can reveal change that one glance cannot.

For an evening search, start with Libra and a current sky chart, then use Brachium’s coordinates to confirm the target. Its southern declination means it appears low in the sky for many northern observers; choose a site with an unobstructed southern horizon. Around June, the Sun is opposite the part of the sky near right ascension 15 hours, making Libra well placed in the evening. Exact rise and set times depend on your location, so check a local planetarium app or star
