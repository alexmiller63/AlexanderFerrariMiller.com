# Aljanah, the Swan’s Quiet Wing

*In Cygnus, a warm-colored giant marks the bird’s outstretched wing—and rewards a closer look.*

On a clear late-summer evening, the Northern Cross rises high in the northern sky. Its brightest star, Deneb, marks the swan’s tail; trace the cross toward Sadr, at its center, and then along the wing. There, Aljanah glows as a steady point of light. It is not the most famous star in Cygnus, but it is bright enough to find without optical aid and interesting enough to linger over.

Aljanah is Epsilon Cygni, also listed in astronomical databases as HIP 102488. SIMBAD identifies it as a double or multiple star and gives its spectral type as K0III: a K-type giant. The classification tells a compact story of stellar change. Its surface is cooler and more orange-toned than the Sun’s, while the “III” indicates a giant star—one that has expanded beyond the main-sequence stage in which it spent most of its life fusing hydrogen in its core. The catalog’s measured parallax places the system roughly 75 light-years away. At that distance, its apparent visual magnitude of about 2.48 makes it an easy naked-eye target from a reasonably dark site.

The “double or multiple” label is a reminder that a star’s appearance depends on how closely we look. To the unaided eye, Aljanah is a single point.
