# Alniyat: A Quiet Pulse Beside the Scorpion’s Heart

*Near brilliant Antares, this blue-white star is cataloged as a Beta Cephei variable—a reminder that even a steady-looking point of light can be changing.*

On a summer evening, find Antares first: Scorpius’s bright, ruddy heart. Nearby, Alniyat shines with a cooler, whiter light. To the unaided eye it looks like a fixed point in the pattern of the Scorpion. Its catalog entry tells a more active story: Alniyat, also designated Sigma Scorpii, is classified as a Beta Cephei variable. [SIMBAD’s entry](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2080112) lists its visual magnitude at about 2.9 and its spectral type as B1III+B1:V.

That spectral notation describes hot, blue B-type stars and records two components in the catalog’s classification. The variable-star label points to another kind of complexity. Beta Cephei stars undergo small, regular changes in brightness associated with pulsations in their outer layers. The changes are generally too subtle and rapid to follow by casually watching with the naked eye. Alniyat may seem still, but its light carries the signature of motion within the star.

The catalog places it at right ascension 16h 21m and declination −25° 35′ (J2000), in Scorpius, and gives a parallax of 4.68 milliarcseconds. A simple inversion of that parallax suggests a distance of roughly 700 light-years, though the listed measurement has an uncertainty and should be treated as an estimate, not a precise yardstick. The same entry identifies Sigma Scorpii as a Beta Cephei variable; it does not provide a light curve for observers to watch night by night.

For a first look, choose a clear evening when Scorpius is above the southern horizon. From much of the Northern Hemisphere, that means summer, when the constellation remains relatively low in the south. Start with Antares, then scan the nearby pattern for Alniyat. The contrast is part of the pleasure: Antares glows orange-red, while Alniyat is a blue-white star. Let your eyes adapt to the dark, and get away from direct lights if you can.
