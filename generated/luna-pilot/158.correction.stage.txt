# M77: Reading the Record of a Seyfert Galaxy

M77 is one name for NGC 1068, a galaxy whose catalog entry brings together several different kinds of astronomical description. SIMBAD identifies it as a Seyfert 2 galaxy and lists its morphological type as SAb. Those labels describe different aspects of the object: one is its recorded galaxy classification, the other its recorded morphological class. Together, they offer a compact introduction to how astronomers organize information about a distant galaxy.

The coordinates provide a precise way to identify M77 in the sky. SIMBAD gives its J2000 position as right ascension 02h 42m 40.7s and declination −00° 00′ 48″. The declination is close to zero, but the coordinate is more than a convenient address: it lets observers and databases distinguish this object from others and connect measurements made at different wavelengths or times. The position is listed with an optical designation in SIMBAD’s record. (SIMBAD, [NGC 1068](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=NGC%201068).)

The catalog also reports an optical angular size of about 5.50 by 5.09 arcminutes. That measurement describes the galaxy’s apparent extent on the sky, not its physical diameter. The distinction matters: an angular measurement tells us how large an object appears from Earth, while a physical size would require additional information. In M77’s entry, the angular size is attributed to the reference 2023ApJS..269....3M.

Its classification as a Seyfert 2 is another concise label, but it should not be mistaken for a complete account of the galaxy. SIMBAD’s basic data lists M77 as a Seyfert 2 galaxy. The database also records additional object-type classifications from different references, a reminder that astronomical catalogs can preserve labels assigned in separate studies. The available entry gives the classification; it does not, by itself, explain every physical process behind the galaxy’s nucleus.

Numbers in the record add further layers. SIMBAD lists an emission redshift of 0.00348372, with an uncertainty of 0.0005, and a radial velocity of 1,043 kilometres per second, with an uncertainty of 150 kilometres per second. The values are catalog measurements, not interchangeable descriptions: one is a redshift, the other a velocity. The redshift entry is associated with the reference 2022ApJS..261....2K.
