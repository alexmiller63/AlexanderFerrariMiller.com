# NGC 7479: A Barred Spiral With a Busy Heart

*In Pegasus, this faint galaxy offers a quiet reminder that a spiral’s central bar can help channel activity toward its core.*

On an autumn evening, Pegasus climbs into view carrying a galaxy that is easy to overlook. NGC 7479 lies beyond the familiar pattern of the constellation’s Great Square, a dim target whose catalogued coordinates are 23h 04m 56.6s, +12° 19′ 22″. Its light gathers in a broad, faint oval; the more intricate story is written in the structure of its spiral.

Astronomers classify NGC 7479 as a barred spiral galaxy. In this kind of system, stars and gas form an elongated bar across the central region, with spiral arms extending outward. The bar is more than a striking shape: it can help move gas inward, toward the galaxy’s center. SIMBAD also lists NGC 7479 as a Seyfert 2 galaxy, a category of active galaxy whose nucleus emits strongly and whose central regions are partly obscured from our line of sight. The catalog records a visual magnitude of 10.85 and an apparent size of about 5.6 by 2.6 arcminutes—useful clues to the challenge, but not a guarantee of what an eyepiece will show. [SIMBAD’s NGC 7479 entry](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=NGC%207479)

For an observer, the galaxy’s catalogued size sounds generous. In practice, its light is spread across that area, so it can appear fainter than a star of similar total brightness. Under a dark sky, a medium-sized telescope may reveal a soft, elongated patch. The bar and arms are harder prizes; dark skies, patient viewing and averted vision can help, while a photograph or a long-exposure image will show far more structure than the eye usually can.

Begin with Pegasus and a detailed star chart or a telescope’s coordinate display. Center the field near the coordinates above, then use a low-power eyepiece to search before increasing magnification. If the galaxy doesn’t appear at once, give your eyes time to adjust and sweep slowly around the target. Avoid judging the view against images made with long exposures: those collect light over minutes or hours, while your eye is working in real time.

NGC 7479 is especially rewarding as a contrast between appearance and activity. At the eyepiece, it may be little more than a pale smudge. Yet its classification points to a structured galaxy with a central bar and an energetic nucleus. The faint patch is not an empty blur but a distant system of stars, gas and dust, seen at an angle and under the limits of the night sky.

Look for it when Pegasus is well placed on autumn evenings. Even if the arms stay hidden, finding NGC 7479 is a satisfying act of astronomical scale: locating a specific galaxy among the stars, then recognizing that its quiet glow hints at a much more dynamic interior.
