# Delta Scorpii: A Binary Star in the Catalog

*SIMBAD identifies the star as a spectroscopic binary. Its catalog entry also records a spectral type, brightness and measurements of its motion.*

A star’s catalog entry can say more than where to find it. SIMBAD, an astronomical database, identifies HIP 78401 as Delta Scorpii, or “del Sco,” and classifies it as a spectroscopic binary. That phrase describes a system recognized through its spectrum: the light from the stars carries evidence of a pair, even when an observer sees only one point. [SIMBAD: HIP 78401 / Delta Scorpii](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2078401)

The distinction matters. “Binary” does not necessarily mean two stars can be picked out separately through a telescope. In a spectroscopic binary, astronomers use changes in the spectrum—the pattern of light—to study the system. SIMBAD’s classification tells us what kind of object the database identifies Delta Scorpii as; it does not, by itself, provide a visual portrait of two distinct stars.

The entry gives Delta Scorpii a spectral type of B0.3IV. In that notation, “B0.3” is the spectral classification, while “IV” is the luminosity class. The database reports the type with a quality flag of B and cites a 1973 reference. These compact labels are not decorative: they let astronomers summarize and compare stellar classifications without relying on a name alone.

SIMBAD also lists a V-band magnitude of 2.32. That is a cataloged measurement, not a guarantee of how bright the star will appear from every location or on every observing night. The entry includes measurements in other filters, too: U, B, R, I, J, H and K. Each filter records brightness in a different part of the spectrum, giving astronomers more information than a single visible-light value can provide.

The catalog records motion as well as light. Its listed radial velocity is −6.00 kilometers per second, with an uncertainty of 0.5 kilometers per second. The parallax is 6.64 milliarcseconds, with an uncertainty of 0.89. SIMBAD also gives proper-motion components of −10.21 and −35.41 milliarcseconds per year. These are measurements reported in the database, each with its own reference; they are not a complete account of the star’s motion or a direct view of its orbiting companion.

Even the position is presented with precision. SIMBAD gives Delta Scorpii’s J2000 right ascension and declination as 16 hours, 00 minutes, 20.00528 seconds and −22 degrees, 37 minutes, 18.1431 seconds. The coordinates locate the catalog entry
