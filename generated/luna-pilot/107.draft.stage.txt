# Albireo: A Swan’s Two-Colored Beak

*One bright point in Cygnus becomes a pair in a telescope: a gentle lesson in stellar color, classification and the limits of what a view can tell us.*

On a clear summer night, Cygnus rises like a great bird crossing the Milky Way. Follow its long body from Deneb, the Swan’s tail, through the central star Sadr toward the beak. There, Albireo waits: a modest-looking point of light to the unaided eye, but one of the sky’s most rewarding telescopic sights.

A small telescope reveals two stars, often described as gold and blue. Their contrast is the spectacle—not a brilliant flare or a sudden change, but a quiet pairing of warm and cool hues against the dark. Albireo is bright enough to find without optical aid under reasonably dark skies; the transformation from one point to two is part of its charm
