# Merope: A Blue Flame in the Pleiades

*The star is easy to find. Its faint, fan-shaped veil of dust asks for darker skies, patient eyes and a little help from optics.*

Merope is one of the Pleiades’ bright stars: a blue-white point in the small, familiar cluster that many people recognize as the Seven Sisters. Its catalog designation is 23 Tauri, or HIP 17608. The star’s listed visual magnitude is 4.18—bright enough to see without a telescope under a reasonably dark sky—and its spectral classification is B6IV(e), identifying a hot, blue-white star with emission features. SIMBAD gives its parallax as 7.067 milliarcseconds, corresponding to a distance of roughly 460 light-years. [SIMBAD: Merope / 23 Tau](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2017608)

Merope’s fame is not only stellar. Close to the star lies NGC 1435, often called Merope’s Nebula: a delicate patch of interstellar dust made visible by the light it scatters. The dust does not shine like a hot gas cloud. Instead, starlight illuminates the particles, which scatter some of that light toward us. The Pleiades’ hot stars make the scattered light appear blue. The nebula’s close apparent association with Merope makes it a striking example of how a star can reveal material otherwise too faint to notice. [SIMBAD: NGC 1435](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=NGC%201435)

Start your search with the Pleiades in Taurus. The cluster is a compact, naked-eye group, but the number of stars you can pick out depends on your eyesight, the darkness of the sky and how long you let your vision adjust. Merope is one of the brighter points within the cluster. For an exact position, SIMBAD lists it at right ascension 03h 46m 19.6s and declination +23° 56′ 54″ (J2000). A current star chart or planetarium app can help orient you, especially if the cluster is high overhead.
