# Imai, the Southern Cross’s Quietly Restless Star

*At the heart of Crux, a bright blue-white star offers an easy naked-eye landmark—and a reminder that even apparently steady lights can change.*

On an autumn evening in the Southern Hemisphere, the Southern Cross stands clear of the horizon, its four bright stars outlining a compact shape. One of them is Imai, also catalogued as Delta Crucis and HIP 59747. At about magnitude 2.75 in visible light, it is readily seen without optical aid from a reasonably dark site. Yet the star has a less obvious distinction: astronomical catalogues classify it as a pulsating variable.

That label does not mean Imai visibly flashes or winks. Stellar pulsation is a change in a star’s size and surface conditions as it oscillates. Those changes can alter its brightness, sometimes by too little—or too slowly—for the unaided eye to notice. SIMBAD lists Imai as a pulsating variable, but its entry does not provide a pulsation period or a visual range. So a casual observer should not expect to watch it brighten and fade over the course of a single night.

The catalogue gives Imai a spectral classification of B2IV: a hot, blue-white star in the subgiant luminosity class. It lists a visual magnitude of 2.752 and a parallax of 7.168 milliarcseconds.
