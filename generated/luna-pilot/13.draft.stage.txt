# Menkar: A Quiet Beacon in the Whale

*An autumn guide to Cetus’s red giant—and to a star whose brightness may not be quite as settled as it looks.*

On an autumn evening, the whale arrives slowly. Cetus spreads across a dim stretch of sky south of Pisces and Aries, its outline easy to lose among the stars. But near the creature’s head, Menkar holds a steady-looking place: a warm point of light with a name that sounds like something spoken across a long distance.

Menkar is Alpha Ceti, though the Greek letter does not mean it is Cetus’s brightest star. Its position is a more reliable guide: about 3 hours 2 minutes right ascension and 4 degrees north declination. The SIMBAD astronomical database identifies it as a type M1.5 giant star and lists a parallax of 13.09 milliarcseconds. That parallax places it roughly 250 light-years away. [SIMBAD: Menkar / HIP 14135](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2014135)

The M in its spectral class tells us Menkar is relatively cool at the surface, giving it a reddish hue; the Roman numeral III marks it as a giant. A giant has expanded far beyond the compact star it once was. Menkar’s light, filtered through that cooler outer atmosphere, reaches us as a golden-orange glimmer—subtle to the eye, more apparent when compared with nearby, whiter stars.

There is a note of uncertainty in its catalogue entry. SIMBAD lists a V-band magnitude of 2.53, but also classifies Menkar as a *long-period variable candidate*. That is a cautious label, not a promise of a dramatic or easily timed change. Still, it lends the observation a little suspense: the star’s apparent brightness may not be entirely fixed. If you return to it over several nights, compare it with nearby stars rather than relying on memory. The eye is a poor photometer, but repeated looks are part of the pleasure.

To find Menkar, begin with the broad sweep of Cetus below Pisces and Aries. A sky map or planetarium app can help trace the constellation’s head; look for Alpha Ceti toward its northern part. At mid-northern latitudes, Menkar is well placed on autumn evenings, climbing into view in the east and standing higher as the night advances. Its coordinates are useful if the surrounding pattern is hard to read: enter **03h 02m, +04°** into a digital chart.
