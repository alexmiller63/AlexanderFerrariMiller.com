# Eta Tauri: A Star in the Catalogue

*One star can be described in several ways: by its name, its position, its motion and the measurements recorded in different bands of light. Eta Tauri’s catalogue entry offers a compact tour of that vocabulary.*

SIMBAD resolves the identifier HIP 17702 to **eta Tau** and lists the object type as a Be star. Its spectral type is given as **B7III**. Those labels are not interchangeable: one is the object type recorded in the database, while the other is the spectral classification. Together, they are the opening lines of a portrait written not in images, but in astronomical data. [SIMBAD: Eta Tauri / HIP 17702](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2017702)

The record places eta Tauri at right ascension 03h 47m 29.0765529s and declination +24° 06′ 18.488347″, in coordinates for the J2000 reference frame. SIMBAD also gives an uncertainty for that position. These coordinates serve as a precise address in the sky; they are more informative than a name alone when the task is to identify an object in a database or compare records.

The listing includes motion as well as position. Its proper motion is 19.34 milliarcseconds per year in right ascension, with the cosine-of-declination factor included, and −43.67 milliarcseconds per year in declination. The figures make clear that the catalogue is not simply pinning the star to a fixed point: it records a measured change in its apparent position over time. SIMBAD attaches a reference to the measurement, as it does to several other values in the entry.
