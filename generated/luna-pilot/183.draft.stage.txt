# Collinder 87: The Small Gathering in Gemini

*An understated open cluster offers a lesson in scale: a compact patch of light can mark a much larger family of stars.*

Some deep-sky objects announce themselves. Collinder 87 asks you to look carefully.

Also cataloged as NGC 2194, this open cluster lies in Gemini, at coordinates 6h 13m 45.6s, +12° 48′ 47″. Its listed apparent diameter is about 9 arcminutes—roughly a third of the Moon’s width. That small footprint is a useful reminder: the sky compresses distance into a flat picture. A star cluster that fits into a modest eyepiece field is not necessarily a small thing in space.

An open cluster is a group of stars formed together from the same cloud of gas and dust. Its members share a history, though gravity does not bind every open cluster forever. Over time, encounters with other stars and the Milky Way’s wider gravitational field can loosen the group, scattering its members into the galaxy. Collinder 87’s SIMBAD entry classifies NGC 2194 as an open cluster and lists a parallax of 0.263 milliarcseconds—a tiny apparent shift that hints at the object’s great distance. The entry also gives a radial velocity of about 8.6 kilometers per second. These measurements are not visible in an eyepiece, but they turn a faint patch into a place with measurable motion and depth. ([SIMBAD: NGC 2194](
