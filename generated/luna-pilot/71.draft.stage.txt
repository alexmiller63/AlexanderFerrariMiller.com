# Eta Ursae Minoris: A Quiet Wanderer in the Little Bear

*Look beyond the famous stars of Ursa Minor and you’ll find a modest, moving point of light—one that rewards patience more than magnification.*

The Little Dipper is easy to claim as a familiar shape and harder to trace in a bright sky. Polaris announces the handle’s end; the bowl’s other stars are less insistent. Eta Ursae Minoris is a subtler member of the constellation, shining at about fifth magnitude. It is not a showpiece, but it has a story written in its slow drift across the sky.

The SIMBAD database identifies Eta Ursae Minoris as an F5V star—a main-sequence star, broadly similar in kind to the Sun, though hotter. Its measured parallax is 33.419 milliarcseconds. Inverting that value gives a distance of about 30 parsecs, or roughly 98 light-years. SIMBAD also lists a radial velocity of −11 kilometres per second, meaning the star is approaching the Sun along our line of sight.
