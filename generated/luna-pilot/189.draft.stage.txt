# Thor's Helmet: A Celestial Visor in Canis Major

*NGC 2359 is a cloud of glowing gas whose mythic outline comes into view best under dark skies—and with patient looking.*

Winter’s bright stars make a fine trailhead. Find Sirius, the brightest star in the night sky, then look roughly half an hour of right ascension east and a few degrees north. There lies NGC 2359, the nebula known as Thor’s Helmet. Its SIMBAD coordinates are 07h 18m 30s, −13° 13′ 36″, and the database classifies it as an HII region: a cloud of gas in which hydrogen has been ionized. [SIMBAD: NGC 2359](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=NGC%202359)

The nickname is a promise of shape, not a guarantee of spectacle. In photographs, arcs and knots of luminous gas gather into a rounded form with sweeping extensions, suggesting a helmet with a crest. At the eyepiece, the nebula is subtler: a faint patch against a field of stars, its edges and inner structure difficult to separate at first. The contrast between camera and eye is part of the object’s character. A long exposure can collect light that a brief glance cannot.
