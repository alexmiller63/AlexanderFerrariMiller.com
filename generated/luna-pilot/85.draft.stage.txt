# Sargas at the Scorpion’s Hinge

*Trace Scorpius’s hooked tail to find a bright yellow-white giant—and a star whose low northern passage makes a clear horizon essential.*

On a June evening, Scorpius climbs into the southern sky like a question mark made of stars. Its red heart, Antares, is hard to miss; the curved tail sweeps away from it toward the horizon. At the tail’s bend shines Sargas, a steady point of light that rewards anyone willing to look low and south.

Sargas is the traditional name for Theta Scorpii, also catalogued as HIP 86228. Its position—right ascension 17h 37m, declination −43°—puts it deep in the southern sky. The star’s apparent magnitude is about 1.85, bright enough to see without binoculars under a reasonably dark sky. In SIMBAD, its spectral type is listed as F1III: an F-type giant, hotter and more luminous than the Sun.
