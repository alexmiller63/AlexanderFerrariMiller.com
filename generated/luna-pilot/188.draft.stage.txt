# M50: A Winter City in Monoceros

*In the faint constellation of the Unicorn, a binocular sweep from Sirius can find a gathering of stars born together—and still traveling as a family.*

Winter’s brightest stars make an easy parade across the evening sky. But between the familiar landmarks of Orion, Sirius and Procyon lies Monoceros, the Unicorn: a constellation too faint to announce itself, yet rich in deep-sky treasures. One of them, M50, is a loose, starry gathering that rewards a small change of direction—and a pause to consider what a cluster really is.

M50 is also catalogued as NGC 2323. SIMBAD classifies it as an open cluster and gives its position as right ascension 7 hours 2 minutes 44 seconds, declination 8 degrees 22 minutes south. Its listed apparent diameter is about 31.6 arcminutes, almost the width of the full Moon. That is an estimate of the cluster’s visible extent, not a sharply drawn boundary: its stars fade into the crowded background of the Milky Way. ([SIMBAD: NGC 2323](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=NGC%202323))
