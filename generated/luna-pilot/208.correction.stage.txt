# NGC 2683: A Galaxy in the Catalog

*Its catalog record gives a compact portrait in coordinates, apparent size and classification—but each number answers a different question.*

A galaxy can be introduced by an image, a name or a set of measurements. For NGC 2683, the SIMBAD astronomical database offers the last of these: a position in the sky, an optical angular size, a morphological classification and a recorded redshift. Together, these details identify the object and describe how it is represented in the catalog. They do not, by themselves, provide a complete account of the galaxy.

SIMBAD gives NGC 2683’s J2000 coordinates as right ascension 08 hours 52 minutes 41.3 seconds and declination +33 degrees 25 minutes 18.6 seconds. Coordinates function as a precise address on the celestial sphere. They tell a reader where the object is located in the catalog’s chosen reference system, rather than describing its physical dimensions or appearance. [SIMBAD: NGC 2683](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=NGC%202683)

The same record lists an optical angular size of 8.71 by 2.57 arcminutes, with an orientation angle of 44 degrees. The two measurements describe an elongated outline: the first is about 3.4 times the second. That ratio is a straightforward comparison of the cataloged apparent dimensions. It should not be mistaken for a measurement of the galaxy’s true size. Angular size describes how much of the sky an object covers as seen from Earth; the entry does not provide a physical diameter alongside these figures.

The catalog also records NGC 2683’s morphological type as “SA.” That is the classification shown in SIMBAD, rather than a detailed description of every feature within the galaxy. The database separately identifies the object as an active-galaxy-nucleus object. These labels belong to different parts of the record: one gives a morphological classification, while the other identifies an additional cataloged property. Neither label, on its own, tells a reader what details would be visible in a particular observation.

A further number in the entry is a spectroscopic redshift of 0.001372. SIMBAD presents it as a measured catalog value, along with a radial velocity entry of 411 kilometers per second. The figures are not a direct photograph of the galaxy; they are measurements recorded in the database. The information cited here does not give a distance for NGC 2683, so the redshift should not be treated as a distance measurement in this account.

This distinction between data and interpretation is useful when reading any astronomical listing. Coordinates locate an object; angular dimensions summarize its apparent extent; a morphology field records a classification; and redshift and velocity fields report measurements.
