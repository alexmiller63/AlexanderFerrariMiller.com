# Sigma Librae: a star worth revisiting

*Cataloged as a Mira variable, this star in Libra offers a simple observing project: find it, note its appearance and return to compare.*

A star can be a useful target even when a catalog cannot tell you exactly what you will see tonight. Sigma Librae—listed in SIMBAD as “sig Lib” and identified there as a Mira variable—invites repeated observation. Its classification marks it as variable, but the catalog record supplied here gives no current brightness forecast or cycle length. The best approach is to treat each sighting as one point in a longer record. [SIMBAD: HIP 73714](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2073714)

SIMBAD gives the star’s position as right ascension 15 hours, 04 minutes, 04.2 seconds and declination 25 degrees, 16 minutes, 55 seconds south, in coordinates for epoch J2000. These numbers are a precise way to confirm a target: enter them into a planetarium app or compare them with a chart of Libra. The same record lists a parallax of 12.5387 milliarcseconds. Parallax is the apparent shift of a star’s position as Earth moves around the Sun; using the catalog value gives a distance of roughly 80 parsecs, or about 260 light-years. [SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2073714)

The catalog also gives a spectral type of M2.5 III. The “M” identifies its spectral class, while “III” is its luminosity class, indicating a giant star. Those labels describe the star; the Mira-variable designation points to why an observer might return to it. A single observation can establish what it looks like on one night, but only repeated estimates can show whether its apparent brightness has changed.

SIMBAD lists a visual magnitude of 3.21, along with measurements in other bands, including B at 4.91 and R at 1.60. These are catalog entries, not a promise of what the star will look like during your next observing session.
