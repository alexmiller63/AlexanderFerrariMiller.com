# Electra: A Blue Spark in the Pleiades

*In the familiar star cluster, a third-magnitude beacon offers a close-up lesson in stellar classification—and an easy target for autumn and winter observers.*

On a clear evening, the Pleiades can look like a tiny dipper spilled across Taurus. Electra is one of its bright points: a blue-white star known in catalogs as 17 Tauri, or HIP 17499. Its light reaches us from a star listed at about magnitude 3.7, bright enough to see without optical aid under a reasonably dark sky. [SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=HIP%2017499)

The cluster is the doorway to finding it. Look east on autumn evenings, or higher in the sky as winter advances, for Taurus and its conspicuous orange star Aldebaran. The Pleiades sit nearby as a compact knot of stars. Give your eyes time to adjust, then look for Electra among the cluster’s brighter members. City lights may hide fainter stars, but Electra can still stand out. Binoculars reveal the Pleiades as a crowded field, though they also make it easier to lose your place among the stars. Try first with your unaided eyes, then sweep the cluster slowly with binoculars.

Electra’s catalog position—right ascension 3 hours 44 minutes 52.5 seconds, declination +24 degrees 06 minutes 48 seconds—can help you locate it with a telescope or a digital sky chart. Its listed parallax is about 8.35 milliarcseconds.
