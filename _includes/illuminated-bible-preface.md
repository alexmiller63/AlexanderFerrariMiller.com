To understand the heart of The Illuminated Bible, one need look no further than the illuminated Bibles of history. Those volumes used art to illuminate the Scriptures. The Illuminated Bible seeks to do the same, while extending that tradition by illuminating the text itself.

Alongside the English text, selected words and phrases from the original Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek appear in their original scripts, with transliteration and IPA pronunciation. Carefully chosen notes explore features of language, poetry, grammar, historical context, and interpretation that cannot be shown naturally within the verse itself. The ancient Syriac tradition of the Peshitta may also be brought into the notes when it provides a useful comparison.

The English text draws principally upon the Bible in Basic English (BBE) and the World English Bible (WEB). Their purpose here is not to obscure the original languages behind polished English, but to provide a clear and readable foundation through which those languages can become visible.

The Illuminated Bible is not intended to replace existing translations. It is intended to illuminate them: to let the reader encounter Scripture as readable English while seeing, hearing, and considering something of the language beneath it.

In this way, The Illuminated Bible continues the ancient tradition of illuminating Scripture—not only by placing art around the words, but by allowing the words themselves to become illuminated.
