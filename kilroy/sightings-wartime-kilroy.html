Wartime Kilroy

Across ships, bases, vehicles, bridges, bathrooms, and battlefields, American servicemen left a worldwide trail announcing that Kilroy had already been there.

Atlantic and Pacific Theaters

The National Park Service records “Kilroy was here” as a mark used by American troops first in the Atlantic Theater and later in the Pacific. It appeared on ship holds, bathrooms, bridges, and even missile shells — less one isolated sighting than a worldwide wartime trail.

Documented · Wartime phenomenonThe mark traveled with American servicemen through both major theaters of World War II.

National Park Service source

Bastogne, Belgium

War photographer Robert Capa recorded a Battle of the Bulge variation on the charred wall of an abandoned barn: “KILROY WAS STUCK HERE.” The joke fit the encircled American position at Bastogne and is one of the strongest location-specific wartime Kilroy accounts.

Contemporary account · December 1944Capa recorded the variation in Slightly Out of Focus.
