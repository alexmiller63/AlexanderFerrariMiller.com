{% capture manuscript %} # For Signs and for Seasons > **Genesis 1:14 — Illuminated** > > God said, “Let there be lights in the expanse of the sky to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs **to mark seasons, days, and years**.” > > **וּלְמוֹעֲדִים וּלְיָמִים וְשָׁנִים** > *ulemo'adim uleyamim veshanim* > **Genesis 1:14 — WEB** > > God said, “Let there be lights in the expanse of the sky to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs to mark seasons, days, and years.” ### Footnote The Hebrew phrase **וּלְמוֹעֲדִים וּלְיָמִים וְשָׁנִים** (*ulemo'adim uleyamim veshanim*) joins three ways of reckoning time. *Mo'adim* can mean appointed times, fixed occasions, or seasons; *yamim* means days; and *shanim* means years. The heavens give us observable motions and recurring markers. Human beings use those motions to reckon time. From them we construct days and years, calendars and seasons, local times and universal time. We draw time zones and an International Date Line. These conventions are not the motions of the heavens themselves. They are the systems by which people living on a spherical, rotating world describe those motions consistently. The stories that follow explore the boundary between the two: **Nature gives us motion. We draw the lines.** {% endcapture %}
