Virgo

The maiden who withdrew from Earth

Virgo has carried the figures of harvest and justice, including a goddess who could no longer bear humanity’s violence.

{% capture constellation_story %} Spica marks an ear of grain in the hand of Virgo. The bright star preserves the agricultural image carried by the Maiden, one of the largest figures in the sky. But the identity of the woman holding the grain was never settled once and for all. Ancient writers offered several goddesses. Some associated her with Demeter and the harvest. Others read her as Dike, Justice, or Astraea. The alternatives connect the same figure with what people need from the Earth and what they owe one another. In the account told by Aratus, Justice once lived among human beings. During an earlier age, she could meet people openly and encourage a better way of life. As generations became less just, she withdrew farther from their company. The Bronze Age brought a decisive estrangement. Violence and the making of weapons marked a world she could no longer endure. Justice left Earth for the sky, where the Maiden remained visible at night. It is a striking reason for a constellation to exist. Many celestial figures commemorate a rescue or reward. This one records a departure. The light overhead becomes a reminder of something human society has driven away. That is one interpretation of Virgo, not an explanation that absorbs all the others. The ear of grain can sustain a harvest association without requiring the goddess of justice to become Demeter in every telling. The constellation was a shared image capable of holding different divine identities. Modern astronomy places a far greater population behind the Maiden’s foreground stars. The Virgo Cluster is a large gathering of galaxies, many accessible as faint patches through amateur telescopes. Unlike the line drawing of Virgo, it is a physical association of immense stellar systems. One of its major members is Messier 87, a giant elliptical galaxy. At its center is the supermassive black hole whose surrounding emission and shadow became the subject of the Event Horizon Telescope collaboration’s first released black-hole image. A bright star, an imagined grain stalk and a field of distant galaxies offer very different ways to enter the same region. Spica helps the eye establish the figure; a telescope opens directions in which whole galaxies replace individual foreground points. Virgo’s old story remains human in scale despite that depth. Justice did not vanish because she was forgotten.
