Total lunar eclipse

2032-10-18

What this eclipse means

Earth passes between the Sun and the full Moon, and the entire Moon enters the umbra, Earth’s dark central shadow. Some sunlight still reaches the Moon after passing through Earth’s atmosphere, which preferentially scatters blue light and bends redder light into the shadow. The eclipsed Moon can glow copper or red; its brightness and color vary with atmospheric conditions.

What to look for

Watch Earth’s curved shadow advance during the partial phases. During totality, compare the Moon’s colors and brightness across its disk and notice how nearby stars become easier to see as moonlight fades. Watch the bright rim return as the Moon begins leaving the umbra.

Earth’s shape, written in shadow

More than 2,300 years ago, Aristotle argued in On the Heavens (Book II, chapter 14) that the consistently curved edge of Earth’s shadow on the eclipsed Moon was evidence that Earth is spherical. Watch that curved bite during the partial phases: you are seeing Earth reveal its own shape on the Moon, the same evidence he described. This is a reason to linger over the changing shadow, as well as to note the time of greatest eclipse.

Watching the eclipse

A lunar eclipse is safe to watch directly, with binoculars, or through a telescope. You can see a phase only while the Moon is above your horizon; clouds and twilight also affect the view.
