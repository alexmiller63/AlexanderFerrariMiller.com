Alpha Caeli — the brightest point in a very faint tool — The Engraving Tool's alpha star is an ordinary hydrogen-burning sun whose modest light makes constellation-finding a test of geometry rather than spectacle.

Beta Caeli — a nearby star crossing the Engraving Tool — Caelum's second-lettered star is a fast-moving F star whose changing catalogue position reminds us that constellation lines are only snapshots.

Alpha Camelopardalis — a runaway with an invisible bow — A modest naked-eye point in the Giraffe is a massive supergiant driving a fast wind into the material ahead of it.

Beta Mensae — a distant giant over Table Mountain — The Greek letter suggests a prominent marker, but Beta Mensae is a fifth-magnitude giant in one of the faintest southern constellations—a useful reminder that a Bayer letter is a label, not a ranking guaranteed by the modern sky.

Beta Camelopardalis — a supergiant measured across a milliarcsecond — Beta Camelopardalis looks like a quiet fourth-magnitude point, yet interferometry has resolved its apparent diameter: several telescopes can combine their light to measure a star no single backyard view can turn into a disk.
