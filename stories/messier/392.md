# Messier catalogued four stars that only look acquainted

M73 is not a star cluster at all, but a tiny asterism whose stars happen to lie along nearly the same line of sight.

That makes it one of the most instructive oddities in the Messier catalog. Early telescopic observers could see a compact little group, but appearance alone could not establish whether its stars were physically associated. Modern measurements of stellar motion and distance let astronomers ask a question Messier could not settle from the eyepiece: are these stars actually traveling through space together?

The answer is no. M73 is a chance alignment.

That does not make it a mistake or a useless object. Quite the opposite. It preserves a basic problem of observational astronomy: the sky is a two-dimensional projection of a three-dimensional universe. Things that look grouped together may be separated by enormous distances.

M73 is therefore a catalog object whose most interesting property is that it is not the kind of object it once appeared to be.
