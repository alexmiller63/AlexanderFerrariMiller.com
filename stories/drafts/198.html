NGC 2440: A Catalog Portrait of a Planetary Nebula

the catalogue identifies NGC 2440 as a planetary nebula and records its position, apparent size and measurements across several bands. The entries offer a compact portrait of the object—and a reminder to read catalog values in context.

NGC 2440 appears in the catalogue under the object type “Planetary Nebula.” Its listed J2000 coordinates are right ascension 7h 41m 55.346s and declination −18° 12′ 30.412″. The coordinate entry is marked as a mid-infrared measurement and carries a quality flag of D, which the catalogue defines as a positional uncertainty of 1 to 10 arcseconds. The record also supplies galactic coordinates: 234.8374° and +2.4211°.

The database gives NGC 2440 an angular size of about 0.364 by 0.340 arcminutes, with an orientation angle of 68 degrees. Converted to arcseconds, those dimensions are roughly 21.8 by 20.4. The catalogue labels the size measurement as radio data and associates it with the 2021 paper identified by the bibcode 2021MNRAS.503.2887B. That label matters: the recorded dimensions are a measurement tied to a particular wavelength category and reference, not a claim that every observation will show the same boundary.

The record also contains five photometric entries: B magnitude 11.469, V magnitude 10.104, and values of 10.202 in g, 10.069 in r and 12.074 in i. Each comes with a listed uncertainty, ranging from 0.01 to 0.10 magnitudes. These numbers are database measurements in named bands; on their own, they do not describe the nebula’s appearance in a telescope or explain how its brightness changes across the object.

The catalogue lists a radial velocity of 62.7 kilometers per second, with an uncertainty of 2 km/s, and a redshift of 0.000209, with an uncertainty of 0.000007. The entry assigns the velocity measurement a quality flag of E and cites a 1953 reference. Keeping those qualifications alongside the values gives a more faithful account than presenting the figures as exact or as measurements from a recent study.

The object has several identifiers in the database, including HD 62166 and PN G234.8+02.4. Such labels help connect records made in different catalogs. But identification requires care: the catalogue notes that NGC 2440 was misidentified with Gaia DR3 5716990599319158016 in a 2022 study. The warning is a useful part of the record, not an incidental detail; a catalog match should not be treated as certain simply because a name appears beside another catalog’s entry.

Taken together, these entries make NGC 2440 more than a name and a set of coordinates. They show how the catalogue assembles information with different origins: a mid-infrared position, a radio angular-size measurement, photometry in several bands and a velocity cited to an older source. The database classifies the object as a planetary nebula, while its individual measurements come with their own references and qualifications. That combination—classification plus traceable, carefully labeled data—is the clearest picture this record supports.
