NGC 1261: Reading a Southern-Sky Catalog Entry

*Its catalog record offers a precise position, an apparent size and brightness, and several measurements that help describe this globular cluster.*

NGC 1261 is identified in SIMBAD as a globular cluster. The entry does not present it as a dramatic naked-eye landmark; instead, it gives observers and researchers a set of coordinates and measurements for locating and describing the object. Taken together, those numbers make a useful portrait of a target far south in the sky. [SIMBAD’s NGC 1261 entry](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=NGC%201261)

The listed J2000 position is right ascension 03h 12m 16.21s and declination −55° 12′ 58.4″. Right ascension and declination are the celestial equivalents of longitude and latitude: the first locates an object around the sky, while the second gives its position north or south of the celestial equator. NGC 1261’s strongly negative declination places it in the far southern sky. As a result, it can rise above the horizon for observers south of roughly 35° north latitude; farther north, it remains below the horizon.

That coordinate is more useful than a verbal direction when planning an observation. A sky map or telescope setting can be matched to the position, while the declination gives a quick sense of whether the cluster is accessible from a given location. SIMBAD labels the coordinate as optical and gives it a quality flag of D, which the database defines as a positional uncertainty of 1 to 10 arcseconds. The listed coordinate is tied to a 2010 reference.

The same entry lists a visual magnitude of 8.63 and an angular size of 6.9 by 6.9 arcminutes. Magnitude is a measure of apparent brightness, while arcminutes describe an angle on the sky: 60 arcminutes make one degree. These are catalog values, not a promise that the cluster will look equally bright or sharply bounded in every observation. They provide a baseline for comparing it with other objects and for setting expectations about its apparent scale.

SIMBAD’s classification supplies the central description: NGC 1261 is a globular cluster. The database’s measurements add detail without, by themselves, telling the whole story of what an observer will see. Its listed apparent size, for example, is an angular measurement, not a physical diameter. The entry identifies the angular-size measurement as optical and links it to a 1989 reference.

The record also includes motion and distance-related measurements. It gives a proper motion of 1.61 milliarcseconds per year in right ascension and −2.05 milliarcseconds per year in declination, with a 2019 reference
