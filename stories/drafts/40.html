Iota Carinae: A Measured Light in the Southern Sky

Catalogued as Iota Carinae, this bright star can be described through a handful of measurements: its position, apparent brightness, spectrum and motion.

A star does not need a long story in its catalogue to be worth finding. Iota Carinae—listed by the catalogue as “iot Car”—is a star in the southern sky, at a declination of about 59 degrees south. Its listed visual magnitude is 2.26, bright enough to make it a naked-eye object under suitable viewing conditions. The number describes how bright it appears from Earth; it does not, on its own, tell us how much light the star produces intrinsically.

For locating it on a chart, the catalogue gives its J2000 position as right ascension 9 hours, 17 minutes, 5.4 seconds and declination −59 degrees, 16 minutes, 31 seconds. These coordinates are a precise address in the sky, rather than a description of the star’s appearance. The catalogue also lists a parallax of 4.26 milliarcseconds, with an uncertainty of 0.10 milliarcseconds. Converting that parallax into a distance gives an estimate of roughly 235 parsecs, or about 765 light-years. The distance is therefore an inference from the measured parallax, not a direct measurement in light-years.

The catalogue records the star’s spectral type as A7 Ib. This compact classification carries more information than a simple color label: “A7” is the spectral subclass, while “Ib” is the luminosity class. The designation places the star among the bright supergiants. It is a catalogue classification, not a complete account of the star’s structure or history, and the entry does not provide a narrative of how it formed or how it will change.
