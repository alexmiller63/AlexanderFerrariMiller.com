Cor Caroli: A bright point with a peculiar spectrum

A star can look like a simple point of light while its catalog entry records a much more specific identity. The catalogue record examined here is for HIP 63125, which the database lists as **alf02 CVn**, or Alpha² Canum Venaticorum. It supplies a sky position, measurements of brightness and motion, and a spectral classification: information gathered in different ways and useful for describing different aspects of the same object.

The catalogue gives the object’s J2000 coordinates as right ascension 12h 56m 01.67s and declination +38° 19′ 06.16″. Coordinates are a precise way to locate a target on a sky map; they do not, by themselves, tell an observer how bright it will appear. For that, the catalog lists a V-band magnitude of 2.88. Magnitudes run in the reverse direction from ordinary brightness rankings, so a lower number means a brighter appearance
