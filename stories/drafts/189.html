Thor’s Helmet: A Celestial Visor in Canis Major

*NGC 2359 has a familiar nickname and a precise catalog identity. The catalogue records its position, classification and several other names—different ways of pointing to the same object.*

A name can make an object feel recognizable before you know much about it. NGC 2359 is widely known as Thor’s Helmet, a nickname that evokes a shape. But in the catalogue, the entry begins with a more formal description: HII Region. The record gives the object’s coordinates and connects its NGC designation to several other identifiers. Together, those details show how astronomers organize the sky: by position, classification and names that have accumulated across catalogs.

The catalogue lists NGC 2359’s optical coordinates in the ICRS system, at epoch J2000, as right ascension 07h 18m 30.000s and declination −13° 13′ 36.00″. These numbers are the object’s address on the celestial sphere. Right ascension measures position around the sky, while declination describes position north or south of the celestial equator. The database also gives a Galactic-coordinate position: longitude 227.760404° and latitude −0.129827°.

A coordinate is not a nickname or a description. It is a way to specify where an object lies, and the coordinate system matters: the same location can be expressed in different reference frames. The catalogue identifies the listed equatorial coordinates as ICRS, with the epoch noted as J2000. The entry assigns those coordinates a quality flag of D. On the page, the catalogue defines D as a positional uncertainty of 1 to 10 arcseconds, while noting that the category also includes some older data. That qualification is a reminder that a database entry is not just a pin on a map; it also records how confidently that pin is placed.

The classification “HII Region” is another kind of information. It identifies the object by type, rather than by where it is. The catalogue explains that object types can originate in the reference cited for an entry, and its page includes other types and naming associations as well. For NGC 2359, the listed identifiers include GUM 4, LBN 227.66−00.09, SH 2-298, GRS G227.80−00.20, LBN 1041 and RCW 5. These are not coordinates or separate objects in this record; they are alternate identifiers associated with NGC 2359.

That collection of labels also explains why searching for a celestial object can involve more than one name. A catalog may preserve the designation used in one survey, while another source uses a different catalog’s numbering system. The catalogue brings those identifiers together under one entry, so a researcher can follow the object across names without assuming that each name signals a different thing.

The record’s reach is broad: the catalogue reports 172 references for NGC 2359, spanning 1850 to 2026, and notes that its bibliographic coverage for objects beyond the Solar System began in 1983. Those figures describe the database’s references, not a complete history of every observation. Still, they suggest how much information can gather around one cataloged object.

Thor’s Helmet is the name that invites the imagination. NGC 2359 is the catalog identity that lets the object be located and cross-referenced. The two serve different purposes: one is memorable; the other is precise enough to connect coordinates, classification and a family of alternate names.
