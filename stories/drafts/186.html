Hubble’s Variable Nebula: Reading the Record

Some astronomical names sound like descriptions, and some like invitations. “Hubble’s Variable Nebula” does both: it names an object and suggests change. In SIMBAD, the object is catalogued as NGC 2261. The database also lists “Hubble’s Var Neb” and “Hubble’s Nebula” among its identifiers, linking those names to the same entry. [SIMBAD: NGC 2261](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-id?Ident=NGC%202261)

That entry gives a precise place to begin: right ascension 6 hours, 39 minutes, 9.95 seconds; declination +8 degrees, 44 minutes, 9.5 seconds, in the J2000 reference frame. These coordinates do not describe what the nebula looks like. They do something more basic: turn a name into a position on the sky, one that can be entered into a chart or observing tool.

The catalogue's basic data identify NGC 2261 with V* R Mon, or R Monocerotis, and classify the star as a Herbig Ae/Be star. The same page gives its spectral type as B8IIIe. These labels are pieces of a record
