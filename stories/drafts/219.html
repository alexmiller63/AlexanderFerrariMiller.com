NGC 3184: Reading a Galaxy Through Its Catalog

*Its catalog entry offers a precise position, an optical size and measurements whose differing quality flags are part of the story.*

A galaxy can be known by a name, a position and a set of measurements, but those details do not always line up neatly. A the catalogue search for NGC 3184 returns a basic-data heading that reads “NGC 3180 — Galaxy towards a Group of Galaxies.” The entry nevertheless supplies coordinates and measurements for the queried object, NGC 3184. The available record does not establish that this object is MCG 7-21-37, so that identification should not be assumed.

The coordinates listed for NGC 3184 are right ascension 10 hours, 18 minutes, 16.985 seconds and declination +41 degrees, 25 minutes, 27.77 seconds, in the J2000 reference frame. The catalogue identifies these as infrared coordinates and gives the measurement a quality flag of C. The catalog defines C as a positional quality in the range 0.1 to 1 arcsecond. These numbers make the position specific; they are not a description of how the galaxy appears through a telescope.

For its apparent size, the catalogue reports an optical major axis of about 6.03 arcminutes and a minor axis of about 5.65 arcminutes, with an orientation angle of 24 degrees. The dimensions describe an extended object on the sky, rather than a point-like source. They are catalog measurements, not a guarantee that an observer will see a sharply bounded outline. The source also lists a morphological type of “SA,” with a quality flag of C. That is the classification recorded in the entry; the supplied data do not offer a more detailed description of the galaxy’s visible structure.
