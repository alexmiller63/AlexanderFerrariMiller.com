A Star in the Record: HIP 86228

*The catalog entry for Theta Scorpii is a compact portrait in numbers: a position, a measured motion, a spectrum and several estimates of brightness and distance.*

The catalogue identifies HIP 86228 as “tet Sco,” a star. Those short labels are the first clues to how astronomers keep track of a particular object among the many stars in the sky. Its recorded position gives a more precise address: right ascension 17 hours, 37 minutes and 19.12985 seconds; declination 42 degrees, 59 minutes and 52.1808 seconds south. The coordinates are given for the J2000 reference system.

The negative declination places the star south of the celestial equator. But coordinates do more than indicate a general direction: they let observers and databases distinguish one target from another. The catalogue also lists a galactic position, along with an older coordinate entry in the FK4 system. Different coordinate systems and reference dates are ways of describing positions; the catalog’s J2000 ICRS entry offers the most detailed current address shown in this record.

A star’s address is not entirely fixed. The catalogue reports HIP 86228’s proper motion as 5.54 milliarcseconds per year in right ascension, adjusted by the cosine of declination, and −3.12 milliarcseconds per year in declination. The listed uncertainties are 2.01 and 1.42 milliarcseconds per year. These small values describe the star’s apparent change of position across the sky over time. They are not a measure of how fast it moves through space in every direction.
