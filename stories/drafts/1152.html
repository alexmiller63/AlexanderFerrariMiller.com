Dabih: The Southern Crowning Star of Capricorn

A detailed overview of Beta Capricorni, highlighting its characteristics and significance within the constellation.

Dabih, also known as Beta Capricorni, is a prominent star situated in the southern portion of the zodiac constellation Capricornus. Its precise celestial coordinates are approximately right ascension 20.35 hours and declination -14.78 degrees, positioning it securely within the frame of this constellation. Dabih is classified as a figure star, meaning it plays a key role in the traditional asterisms associated with Capricornus, often serving as a navigational marker for observers. As a star in the Bayer catalog, it bears the designation Beta, with the traditional name Dabih derived from Arabic origins, reflecting its historical significance to stargazers across cultures. Typically, Dabih is considered a multiple star system, with its primary component shining as a bright and stable object in the night sky, visible to amateur astronomers without the aid of telescopes. Its luminosity and position make it a notable feature for observers mapping the region of the celestial sphere associated with Capricornus. The star's physical characteristics and precise distance continue to be subjects of scientific interest, yet it remains a reliable fixture in sky charts and star guides. Positioned in an area rich with other notable constellations and celestial objects, Dabih offers both a visual delight and an astronomical point of study, anchoring the figure of Capricorn and contributing to its mythological and navigational heritage. As a bright, stable beacon, it guides celestial explorers and enriches the tapestry of our night sky, embodying both the mystery and order of the cosmos.
