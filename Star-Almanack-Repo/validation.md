# Star Almanack Validation

## Status

The Star Almanack solar-ingress calculations have passed the present stage of validation.

The purpose of this validation is to determine whether the Star Almanack equations correctly identify the boundaries between the twelve tropical zodiac signs: the instants at which the Sun's apparent ecliptic longitude reaches successive multiples of 30°.

## Independent Published Comparisons

The Star Almanack results were compared with independently published solar-ingress calendars from multiple astronomical/astrological sources.

At the precision published by those sources, generally to the nearest minute, the Star Almanack results agree throughout the twelve-sign cycle.

No aberrant boundary was identified. In particular, there was no instance in which a Star Almanack result departed from the independently published ingress times by several minutes, hours, or a calendar date.

This provides a useful independent check that the equations reproduce the established sequence and timing of the tropical zodiac boundaries.

## Present Conclusion

The twelve-anchor test provides sufficient validation for the present stage of development of Star Almanack.

The calculations reproduce independently published solar-ingress times to the precision presently available from the comparison sources.

This should not be interpreted as the final seconds-level validation of the equations.

## Further Validation

A higher-precision validation remains planned.

The twelve Star Almanack boundary times will eventually be compared against independently solved crossings using:

1. JPL Horizons, using the Sun's geocentric apparent ecliptic longitude.

2. IMCCE/INPOP, maintained by the Institut de mécanique céleste et de calcul des éphémérides in France.

These comparisons will determine the signed error of each Star Almanack boundary in seconds and reveal whether any systematic or periodic residual error remains.

Until that higher-precision comparison is completed, the current results should be regarded as provisionally validated to published minute-level precision.
