# Constellations and Asterisms

## 2026-09-07 — Geometric center model

Constellations and asterisms use a common geometric idea: treat the defined sky region as if it were cut out of the celestial sphere and balanced on a finger. The balance point is the spherical area centroid of that region and is the canonical geometric center for visibility calculations and labeling.

### Constellations

A constellation is defined by its official IAU boundary region, not by its stick figure. The constellation center is the spherical area centroid of the IAU boundary patch.

Each constellation may therefore contribute three distinct calculated visibility days:

1. α visibility day — derived from the constellation's legitimate α star or α system.
2. β visibility day — derived from the constellation's legitimate β star or β system.
3. Center visibility day — derived from the spherical centroid of the official IAU boundary region.

These are independent astronomical quantities. If a constellation legitimately lacks α or β, omit that corresponding day rather than inventing a designation.

### Asterisms

Asterisms are curated star patterns rather than IAU regions. Define an asterism geometrically as follows:

1. Select the member stars deliberately.
2. Construct the tightest boundary enclosing the selected stars with zero added margin.
3. Treat that boundary as a region on the celestial sphere.
4. Compute its spherical area centroid — the same balance point used for constellations.
5. Use that centroid for the asterism's own visibility timing and label placement.

Member stars retain their own independent star visibility dates. Asterism membership can qualify a member star for calendar inclusion, subject to observer-target deduplication.
