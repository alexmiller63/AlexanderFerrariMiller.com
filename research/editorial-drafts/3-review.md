# Algenib — the pulsing corner of Pegasus

**Dek:** One corner of the Great Square belongs to a star whose brightness changes as it pulsates.

Begin with the **Great Square of Pegasus**: four conspicuous stars that outline a broad shape in the autumn evening sky. One corner is **Algenib**, Gamma Pegasi (γ Peg; HIP 1067). On conventional north-up star charts it forms the southeastern corner, opposite Scheat.

Algenib is a useful marker because the Square's outline is easier to recognize than the individual constellations around it. Find the four corners first, then use a chart to identify Algenib. You need no telescope to see the star itself under reasonably clear skies.

The star has a scientific story beyond its position. Algenib is a hot, luminous B-type star and a **Beta Cephei variable**. Its outer layers pulsate, causing modest, rapid changes in light. This is a real, established kind of variability—not an unconfirmed possibility, as the recovered pilot draft suggested.

A human eye will not easily track the changes during a casual session. Repeated precision measurements, however, let astronomers investigate oscillations within the star. Algenib illustrates a theme that runs through stellar astronomy: the points that make our constellations seem permanent are physical systems in motion.
