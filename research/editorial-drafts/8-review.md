# Xi Ceti — finding a star in the celestial sea

**Dek:** In the broad constellation Cetus, a Bayer letter and a careful chart turn a catalog entry into a real observing destination.

**Cetus** spreads across a large region of the autumn sky. Its mythological sea creature is harder to trace than the W of Cassiopeia or the Great Square of Pegasus. That makes **Xi Ceti** (ξ Cet; HIP 11484) an opportunity to practice finding a particular star by its catalog identity and position.

Start with a chart set for your date and location. Locate the outline of Cetus, then search for ξ Cet rather than scanning randomly through the constellation. A chart with Bayer labels links the picture on the page to astronomical catalogs.

Xi Ceti shines at roughly **visual magnitude 4.3**, which puts it within naked-eye reach under reasonably dark skies, though city lighting may hide it. Binoculars can help with identification, especially if you compare the surrounding stars against a chart. The recovered pilot's arbitrary recommendation of a four-inch telescope was unnecessary.

Once you identify Xi Ceti, note the neighboring stars that helped you recognize it. That short route—bright guide star, chart field, target—is a repeatable technique for finding less familiar objects throughout the sky.
