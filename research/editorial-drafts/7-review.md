# Segin — Cassiopeia's outer marker

**Dek:** Follow the W to Epsilon Cassiopeiae, a blue-white star with evidence of emission-line behavior.

Look for the five bright points of Cassiopeia. **Segin**, Epsilon Cassiopeiae (ε Cas; HIP 8886), marks an outer end of the W. Because the whole pattern rotates around the north celestial pole, use a finder chart to decide which end is Segin on your observing night.

SIMBAD currently classifies Segin as a **Be star**, and lists a B3-type spectrum with shell-related features. This is stronger evidence than the original draft's unsupported generalizations: it provides a reason to discuss emission-line behavior and circumstellar material, while avoiding a claim that every detail of the disk is directly established for this star. The distinction between catalog classification and a physical model matters.

Segin can still teach a useful observing lesson. Bright stars in a familiar asterism have catalog identities, spectra and distances that can be checked independently. The W is an excellent route into the constellation, but it is a pattern projected on the sky, not a physical chain of neighboring stars.

Find Segin, then compare it with the other four stars of the W. Their different colors and brightnesses hint at the variety of stars hidden within one familiar outline.
