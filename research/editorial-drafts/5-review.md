# Gamma Cassiopeiae — a star with an unsettled X-ray mystery

**Dek:** In the middle of Cassiopeia's W, a hot star surrounds itself with gas and emits unexpectedly energetic X-rays.

Cassiopeia's W has a star near its middle that deserves more than a passing glance. **Gamma Cassiopeiae** (γ Cas; HIP 4427) is bright enough to find with the unaided eye, but the most interesting things about it cannot be seen directly through a backyard telescope.

Gamma Cassiopeiae is the prototype of a class of **Be stars**—hot B-type stars whose spectra show emission from gas around them. A circumstellar disk helps produce prominent hydrogen emission lines. The star also varies in brightness, giving observers with suitable equipment a target for sustained monitoring.

The bigger puzzle is its **unusually strong, hard X-ray emission**. Researchers have proposed more than one explanation, including interactions involving a companion and processes associated with the star and its disk. The origin remains an active research question. It would be misleading to describe Gamma Cassiopeiae as a definitively confirmed high-mass X-ray binary containing a neutron star or black hole, as the recovered draft did.

For a visual observer, the reward is finding the star in the W. For a spectroscopist or photometrist, the reward is the chance to study a changing system whose physics is still being investigated.
